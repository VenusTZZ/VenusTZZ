From silu(gate) * up to a Triton fused kernel

SwiGLU is the standard activation structure in the feed-forward networks of modern large models (LLaMA, PaLM, Mistral, DeepSeek, and others). This page uses hands-on experiments to explain three things: its mathematical structure, how gating works, and why Triton can use a single kernel to eliminate the intermediate tensors and redundant HBM round-trips of eager mode.

out = silu(gate) ⊗ up  , where  silu(x) = x · σ(x)

Note the structural detail: SiLU acts on the content branch while the other branch remains linear, and the two are multiplied element-wise. This is the opposite of the original GLU, where sigmoid acts as the gate, and is the easiest point to mix up.

03

Gating: each channel dynamically decides how much to let through

View the 12 channels side by side: the blue bar is the gate opening σ(gate), and the green bar is the final output |silu(gate)·up|. A negative gate nearly closes the gate and pushes the output toward 0; a positive gate opens it.

Conventional activations such as ReLU apply a fixed-shape transformation to the signal; gating lets the network dynamically learn how far to open each channel based on the current data, which is the source of SwiGLU's expressiveness.

The 5 eager accesses are: read gate, write and read the temporary tensor, read up, write out; the 3 fused accesses are: read gate, read up, write out. Regardless of tensor size, fusion consistently cuts forward HBM traffic by 40% and saves one kernel launch. Backward is analogous: eager uses a chain of temporaries, while the fused backward kernel performs 3 reads and 2 writes.

The forward pass saves only gate and up, not the sigmoid result; the backward pass recomputes it on-chip when needed. Elementwise recomputation is cheap, whereas writing a large tensor to HBM and reading it back is expensive; this is a classic case of trading compute for memory.

Packed layout: one GEMM output, zero-copy reads

gate and up can be produced by a single fused GEMM as a packed [..., 2·I] tensor. The kernel uses integer division to convert output linear indices back into packed addresses, with no chunking or copying.

Backward is analogous: the two gradients are written back to their corresponding addresses in the same packed gradient buffer. Because the custom kernel does not build an autograd graph, the packed path supports first-order gradients only; create_graph=True fails immediately rather than silently returning a wrong result.

08

Takeaways: optimization checklist and limits

AMinimal memory traffic

Forward: 2 reads and 1 write per element; backward: 3 reads and 2 writes. Intermediates stay in registers, eliminating HBM round-trips for temporary tensors.

BHalf the launches

Two elementwise forward kernels become one; with many MoE experts, launch and allocator overhead drop as well.

Cfp32 accumulation

fp16/bf16 input, fp32 compute, storage in the original dtype; numerically stable even for large sigmoid inputs.

DPacked zero-copy

Address arithmetic consumes the fused GEMM's [..., 2I] output in place; constexpr specializations cover standalone, packed, and multiple formula variants.

EHardware dispatch

Dispatch lets each platform override with a specialized implementation, falls back to eager when Triton is unavailable, and keeps the math strictly aligned.

FKnow the limits

elementwise and GEMM remain separate kernels (the ultimate form is GEMM epilogue fusion); contiguity may involve an implicit copy; no second-order gradients.
